# High-Level Project Summary

Smallholder farmers in Guatemala's Dry Corridor plant *milpa* (maize and beans) on rain alone, and a single long *canícula* (mid-summer dry spell) can wipe out a season. We built a decision-support tool that turns NASA Earth observations into a planting and rotation plan for their municipality, delivered where they already are: **WhatsApp**. A farmer sends a location pin; a deterministic engine (FAO-56 water balance on NASA POWER and GPM IMERG data, Monte Carlo over 2001–2026, CMIP6 deltas for 2040) compares three strategies (their current milpa, a bean/sorghum relay in *Postrera*, and a mucuna/canavalia cover crop) with risk ranges, water deficit, soil organic-matter trend, bare-soil periods and the five-point sheet the challenge asks for. Subscribers then receive dry-spell and heavy-rain alerts all season, and a 2026 "time machine" replays the season day by day to show how early the system would have warned (⟦TBD: X days before the 2026 canícula in Jocotán⟧). Extension workers get a web map of risk across Guatemala's 340 municipalities. It supports, never replaces, the farmer and their local extension agent.
